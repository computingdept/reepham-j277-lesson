Published family-friendly overview

September–DecemberStudents begin the GCSE course by learning how computers work. They study the CPU, memory, storage, data representation and networks.

Alongside this, students build coding and problem-solving skills using algorithms, variables, selection, loops, inputs and outputs. Students complete regular short coding tasks so programming confidence builds over time.

Assessment: Paper 1 topics 1.1 and 1.2, and Paper 2 topics 2.1 and 2.2.
January–MarchStudents continue learning how computers communicate and how systems are protected. They study networks, internet services, cyber-security threats and ways to reduce risk.

In Paper 2, students keep practising coding. They also use Boolean logic and learn how programming languages and development tools support programmers.

Assessment: Paper 1 topics 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4, and Paper 2 topics 2.1, 2.2, 2.4 and 2.5.
April–JuneStudents learn about operating systems, utility software and the wider impact of digital technology on people, organisations and society.

Students strengthen Paper 2 skills by writing reliable programs, testing solutions, using trace tables, solving problems and improving written answers.

Assessment: the full Year 10 taught course, including Paper 1 topics 1.1–1.6 and Paper 2 topics 2.1–2.5.

Assessment Point 1 alignment

AP1 is aligned to the published family-friendly SoL: Paper 1 topics 1.1 and 1.2; Paper 2 topics 2.1 and 2.2. Numbers, bitmap images, sound, secondary storage, CPU/registers, algorithms and programming foundations are scheduled before the assessment.
